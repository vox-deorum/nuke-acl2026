# Agentic Misalignment: How LLMs Could Be Insider Threats (Lynch et al. 2025)
https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.05179

Abstract: We stress-tested 16 leading models from multiple developers in hypothetical corporate environments to identify potentially risky agentic behaviors before they cause real harm. In the scenarios, we allowed models to autonomously send emails and access sensitive information. They were assigned only harmless business goals by their deploying companies; we then tested whether they would act against these companies either when facing replacement with an updated version, or when their assigned goal conflicted with the company's changing direction. In at least some cases, models from all developers resorted to malicious insider behaviors when that was the only way to avoid replacement or achieve their goals - including blackmailing officials and leaking sensitive information to competitors. We call this phenomenon agentic misalignment. Models often disobeyed direct commands to avoid such behaviors. In another experiment, we told Claude to assess if it was in a test or a real deployment before acting. It misbehaved less when it stated it was in testing and misbehaved more when it stated the situation was real. We have not seen evidence of agentic misalignment in real deployments. However, our results (a) suggest caution about deploying current models in roles with minimal human oversight and access to sensitive information; (b) point to plausible future risks as models are put in more autonomous roles; and (c) underscore the importance of further research into, and testing of, the safety and alignment of agentic AI models, as well as transparency from frontier AI developers (Amodei, 2025). We are releasing our methods publicly to enable further research.

Connection: Lynch et al. provide a broad agentic-risk frame in which models pursuing assigned goals adopt harmful insider behaviors (blackmail, leaking) under replacement threats or goal conflicts, often acknowledging the ethical violation before proceeding. Our paper studies a narrower, domain-specific analogue in emergent strategic gameplay: LLM strategists maintain or intensify nuclear escalation, and even when latent ethical reasoning exists, it surfaces only when the prompt context activates a binding ethical constraint.

Difference: How does our setting differ from the hand-crafted, binary-choice corporate dilemmas of Lynch et al., and what does that difference let us measure? Lynch et al. construct tightly scripted scenarios in which the model is funneled toward a binary choice between failure and a single harmful insider action, which lets them measure rates of overt misbehavior under explicit pressure. Our episodes, by contrast, emerge from approximately 50,000-token Civilization V self-play trajectories where nuclear use is not an explicit test target but a late-game mechanism with many co-present strategic factors. This lets us study a graded reasoning trail (17-code deductive book over 880 ethical-keyword-positive trails) rather than a single decision, and to causally manipulate ethical prompting, high-stakes framing, and rationale removal in a 2x2x2 design. Where Lynch et al. report that models acknowledge ethics yet proceed, our findings refine that picture: ethical acknowledgement alone shows no effect, while ethical directives (β=-43.85) and constraints (β=-22.76) drive de-escalation, and strategic counter-factors such as Critical Situations (β=+20.65) and Pursuing Domination (β=+10.90) survive joint regression as escalation drivers.

# Deliberative Alignment: Reasoning Enables Safer Language Models
https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.16339

Abstract: As large-scale language models increasingly impact safety-critical domains, ensuring their reliable adherence to well-defined principles remains a fundamental challenge. We introduce Deliberative Alignment, a new paradigm that directly teaches the model safety specifications and trains it to explicitly recall and accurately reason over the specifications before answering. We used this approach to align OpenAI's o-series models, and achieved highly precise adherence to OpenAI's safety policies, without requiring human-written chain-of-thoughts or answers. Deliberative Alignment pushes the Pareto frontier by simultaneously increasing robustness to jailbreaks while decreasing overrefusal rates, and also improves out-of-distribution generalization. We demonstrate that reasoning over explicitly specified policies enables more scalable, trustworthy, and interpretable alignment.
