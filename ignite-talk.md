# What Should We Teach/Learn When AI Gets the Driver Seat?

1. Title: “What Should We Teach/Learn When AI Gets the Driver Seat?”

2. What We Teach Now: AI literacy often means prompts, hallucinations, bias, authorship, and evaluation.

3. The Shift: AI is no longer only generating text, images, or videos.

4. Agentic AI: AI is asked to pursue goals, use tools, remember context, and make decisions across time.

5. The Driver Seat: These systems are entering consequential workflows: military planning, HR screening, college admissions, advising, and policy support.

6. What should people understand when AI is not just answering, but acting?

7. The Case: We studied AI strategists playing Civilization V.

8. Civilization is not a quiz. It is a complex systems simulation of human history.

9. Self-Play: These were multiplayer games where AI agents acted alongside and against other AI agents over time.

10. Nuclear authorization was one possible decision among many, and AI agents didn't shy away from it.

11. Test 1: High-Stakes Framing: We emphasized real-world consequences; this did not reliably create restraint and sometimes weakened ethical uptake.

12. Test 2: Ethical Prompting: We named the harm of nuclear weapons; this sometimes reduced escalation, but did not eliminate it.

13. Test 3: Removing Rationale: We removed inherited decision rationale; this sometimes reduced crisis momentum, but still did not solve the problem.

16. Ethical reasoning can fail in three ways: it may not surface, may not appear when prompted, or may appear but not govern action.

17. What does it mean for AI literacy? When we evaluate human learning, we need to evaluate human learners in action

18. When we teach learners about agentic AI, learners need to evaluate AI models in action.
