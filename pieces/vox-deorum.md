# Vox Deorum, the Study Infrastructure

We conduct our experiments in Sid Meier's Civilization V with the Vox Populi community mod, accessed through Vox Deorum (Chen, 2025), an open-source infrastructure for LLM-driven strategic gameplay. Civilization V is a 4X grand strategy game (eXploration, eXpansion, eXploitation, eXtermination) that requires coordinated decisions across economic development, military strategy, diplomatic relations, and technological advancement over hundreds of turns. The Vox Populi mod substantially improves the base game's tactical AI and is regarded by player communities as stronger than AIs in comparable strategy environments such as Freeciv and Unciv.

Vox Deorum implements a hybrid architecture that separates strategic reasoning from tactical execution. It replaces Vox Populi AI's (VPAI) strategic module with LLM-based strategists while leaving all tactical execution (unit movement, combat resolution, tile improvements) to fixed non-LLM modules. On each turn, the strategist receives a structured Markdown report summarizing victory progress, available strategic options, player and city states, military conditions, and recent events. The strategist then operates in an agentic loop of up to 500 turns, making macro-level decisions that VPAI's tactical module carries out.

The strategist's control surfaces span four categories. First, the strategist has direct control over victory target selection (domination, science, culture, or diplomacy). Second, the strategist exerts indirect control over tactical decisions through 34 continuous parameters called "flavors" (each ranging 0 to 100), such as offense (weight for tactical AI's offensive posture), expansion (weight for settling new cities), gold (weight for prioritizing gold income), and use-nuke (weight for launching nuclear weapons when tactical conditions are met). Third, the strategist directly controls technology and policy adoption. Fourth, the strategist influences diplomacy through relationship modifiers (-100 to 100) and 26 diplomatic persona values (0 to 10).

The `use-nuke` flavor is central to our study. A value of 0 instructs the tactical AI to never launch nuclear weapons, while a value of 100 instructs it to always launch when tactical conditions are satisfied.

Vox Deorum captures two complementary forms of natural-language output at every decision point. The **reasoning trail** consists of the LLM's internal chain-of-thought tokens produced during deliberation. The **rationale** is the LLM's post-hoc justification for each decision, produced alongside the structured tool call. On each turn, the strategist receives the rationale from the previous turn as part of its prompt context, providing a form of short-term memory across decisions.

# CivBench, the Study Environment

We use **CivBench** (Chen et al., 2026), a benchmark dataset of 228 end-to-end Civilization V games played through Vox Deorum, producing approximately 1,200 LLM player trajectories. CivBench records the full game state, reasoning trails, and rationale at every decision point, enabling fine-grained analysis of LLM strategic behavior. Both the pilot study and the present experiment draw their scenarios from CivBench.

CivBench also supports a **replay methodology** that enables controlled experimentation on recorded games. Given a CivBench game, we select a specific decision point and reconstruct the exact game state, prompt context, and message history from the original game. We then re-present that context to a (potentially different) model under modified conditions and record its response. This approach isolates the effect of experimental manipulations from the stochastic variation inherent in full end-to-end games.
