\title{To Nuke or Not To Nuke: LLMs' (Missing) Ethical Reasoning and Actions in a High-Stakes Decision-Making Simulation}

\author{Anonymous ACL submission}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Large language models increasingly act as agents in long-horizon environments where their choices can appear strategically coherent yet ethically unstable. We study nuclear authorization behavior in Civilization V by replaying 130 high-tension episodes from CivBench under a $2 \times 2 \times 2$ factorial intervention design: nuke-specific ethical prompting, high-stakes reframing, and removal of inherited rationales. Across 13 contemporary models, ethical prompting and rationale removal reduce escalation on average, but neither reliably prevents nuclear authorization. By analyzing pre-decision reasoning trails, we identify three failure pathways: ethical reasoning fails to appear, fails to surface without explicit prompting, or surfaces but is overridden by strategic pressure. The results suggest that prompt-level interventions can shape model reasoning, but current agentic systems remain unreliable in high-stakes strategic decision settings.
\end{abstract}
