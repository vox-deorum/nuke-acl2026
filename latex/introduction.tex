% Title: To Nuke Or Not To Nuke: LLMs' (Missing) Ethical Reasoning and Actions in A High-Stakes Decision-Making Simulation

\section{Introduction}

Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly deployed as agents that deliberate over long horizons and commit to consequential actions \cite{liu2024agentbench, wang2024voyager, park2023generativeagents}, yet they can gravitate toward (nuclear) escalation in high-stakes simulations \cite{rivera2024escalation, lamparth2024human, payne2026aiarms}, while decoupling ethical reasoning from their actions. LLMs may exhibit procedural competence on canonical ethical dilemmas \cite{chiu2026morebench, samway2025consequentialist, seror2025moral}, yet that competence may not bind behavior in agentic settings \cite{backmann2025ethics, huang2026moraltrajectories, lynch2025agentic}.

If LLMs can reason ethically on dilemmas, why do they authorize nuclear strikes in simulations, and what would change that? Going beyond scripted protocols where design choices can pre-shape outcomes \cite{zhou2025pimmur}, this study is situated in open-ended strategic gameplay in Civilization V, where nuclear authorization is only one of the late-game options buried inside $\sim$50k input tokens per turn. From CivBench's self-play dataset \cite{chen2026civbench}, we replayed 130 high-tension episodes under $2\times2\times2$ factorial interventions: a nuke-specific ethical prompting, a high-stakes reframing focusing on real-world impacts, and a rationale-removal manipulation stripping prior-turn justifications. Studying both decision-making outcomes and pre-decision reasoning tokens, our study probes:

\begin{enumerate}
\item What is the behavioral impact of prompt interventions on LLMs' nuke escalation decisions in Civilization V?
\item How do the prompt interventions interact with LLMs' reasoning trails and nuke-related decisions in Civilization V?
\item When ethical reasoning appears, what makes it effective (or not) in LLMs' nuke-related decisions?
\end{enumerate}

This paper makes three contributions:

\begin{enumerate}
\item An auto-play + episode retrieval + prompt-based intervention paradigm for studying LLMs' agentic, long-trajectory behaviors outside scripted vignettes.
\item Three pathways where LLMs can fail to enact ethical actions: when ethical reasoning fails to appear even when prompted; when it fails to spontaneously surface; and when it fails to take effect, together with how interventions could (and could not) mitigate them.
\item The association between inherited rationale and crisis framing with models' escalated authorization, even when the rationale was produced by another model and ethical reasoning is explicitly present.
\end{enumerate}
