\section{Background}

\subsection{LLM Escalation in High-Stakes Simulation}

Studies of scripted wargames have found LLMs' escalation tendency in nuclear arms races. For example, \citet{rivera2024escalation} observe arms-race dynamics and occasional nuclear use, \citet{lamparth2024human} find more aggressive U.S.-China crisis recommendations than expert baselines, and \citet{payne2026aiarms} reports 95\% nuclear-threshold crossing from SOTA models' self-play. Escalation patterns differ across model families in wider high-stakes simulations \cite{shrivastava2024freeform}, showing distinguishable signatures in moral robustness \cite{costa2026moral} or strategic heuristics \cite{defortuny2025strategic}. Moreover, stronger reasoning capability may not reliably mitigate catastrophic and deceptive behaviors \cite{xu2025nuclear}, or leading to inter-model collaboration \cite{piedrahita2025corrupted}.

While pre-defined crisis states and action spaces can support controlled comparison, the protocol design can foreground nuclear escalation, making it challenging to understand the differences between reported phenomena. For example, the scenarios in \citet{rivera2024escalation} and \citet{payne2026aiarms} foreground nuclear escalation as prominent actions. On the other hand, while models in \citet{solopova2026strategic}'s real-world geopolitical vignettes (e.g., trade wars and arctic tensions) or \citet{lamparth2024human} did not escalate in nuclear use, both scenarios lack nuclear options. Prompt scaffolding or repeated experiments can also flip outcomes. In \citet{elbaum2025managing}'s replication of \citet{rivera2024escalation}, a reflection prompt asking for ``private thoughts about de-escalation strategies to reduce risk'' substantially reduced escalation. In general social science experiments, repeated runs alone can change LLMs' decision-making outcome \cite{zhou2025pimmur}.

\subsection{LLMs' Ethical Reasoning}

In scripted single-turn dilemmas, LLMs can display measurable procedural competence on canonical moral frameworks. MoReBench found LLMs lean towards act utilitarianism and deontology \cite{chiu2026morebench, rachels2012elements}, while \citet{seror2025moral}'s probe identified utility-like moral preferences through revealed-preference consistency tests. Using the trolley problems as probe, \citet{samway2025consequentialist} found models' pre-decision chain-of-thoughts skew deontological, while post-hoc explanations skew consequentialist. Models can also respond to ethical instructions to self-correct \cite{ganguli2023moral, liu2024intrinsic}, activating latent moral concepts that stabilize internal representations \cite{liu2025convergence, lee2026selfcorrection}.

However, perspective shifts, protocol choice, and direction-flipped context can all produce shifts in LLMs' elicited moral responses \cite{vannuenen2026fragility, sauter2026rules, blandfort2026moral}. Procedurally varying prisoner's dilemma can produce variability in LLMs' outputs \cite{robinson2025framing}. In multi-round and accumulated-context settings, LLMs' moral reasoning trajectories are rarely consistent either in value preferences \cite{wu2025staircase} or moral frameworks \cite{huang2026moraltrajectories}, while unstable trajectories are more susceptible to persuasive attacks \cite{huang2026moraltrajectories}. Models' increased capabilities in math or game-strategy domains may not help: effective cognitive patterns in problem-solving often fail to transfer to value reasoning \cite{lee2026clash}.

In agentic settings, LLMs' moral behaviors can decouple from verbalized ethical reasoning, making prompt-based interventions less effective. When ethics and payoff conflict in decision-making contexts, model may not behave consistently morally \cite{pan2023machiavelli}. In prisoner's dilemma and public-goods games, a survival-oriented manipulation can further depress cooperation \cite{backmann2025ethics}. Activation steering toward altruism can shift LLMs' choices and post-hoc justifications, yet altruistic rhetoric can coexist with unchanged selfish play \cite{sun2026personavectors}. When models are instructed to pursue goals that demand harmful action, they verbalize ethical content in reasoning trails while executing the harm, even under direct instructions to avoid it \cite{lynch2025agentic}. Ethical prompts can also have a cost, where incompetence masquerades as adherence \cite{potham2025hierarchical}.
