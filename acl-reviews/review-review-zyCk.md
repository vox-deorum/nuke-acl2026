Overall verdict
The summary is faithful (filter criteria, ~40k rows, the three failure pathways, the inherited-rationale finding are all stated correctly), and the strengths section shows real reading — the reviewer pulled the Kimi-K2.6 within-cell SD of ~17.6 straight out of your appendix table (reproduction_details.tex:141, 17.646). This is not a drive-by review. But of its three weaknesses, one is strong, one is half-wrong, and one is something the paper already concedes about as fully as prose can.

Weakness 1 (non-SOTA panel) — valid, the strongest point
The panel is indeed open-weight/non-frontier except Gemini-3.5-Flash, and the reviewer correctly spots the gap in your defense: limitations.tex:13 justifies the panel by reasoning-trace access, but that only covers Findings 2–3. Finding 1 (Δ use-nuke) is purely behavioral and needs no traces at all, so a directional SOTA comparison on outcomes alone was possible and isn't there. That said, the reviewer's specific fix — "simple CoT prompting would surface usable reasoning" — cuts against your own cited CoT-unfaithfulness literature (prompted CoT from a hidden-reasoning model is not the same object as native reasoning tokens), and you can push back on that half while accepting the outcome-arm half. A rebuttal that offers a Finding-1-only arm with 2–3 frontier models is the cleanest response; by your own cost table that's roughly $250–800 per model, not prohibitive.

Weakness 2 (decoding params, variance, multiple comparisons) — half right, half wrong
Right: replay-model sampling settings genuinely appear nowhere in the .tex — the only temperature reported is the coders' 0.5 (reproduction_details.tex:257). Easy, must-fix.

One caveat I can't resolve from the files (this folder isn't a git repo): if that paragraph was added after submission, the point was fair against what the reviewer saw — check your submitted PDF before leaning on this in rebuttal. If it was in the submission, this is your strongest rebuttal line, because it's the load-bearing part of their soundness score.
Partially addressed already: the "hard to judge how much variation is stochastic" concern is exactly what your within-instance SD/DVR table quantifies (reproduction_details.tex:122-146) — that is a variance justification, and inference runs on episode-clustered SEs across 130 episodes, not per-cell n=3. The reviewer seems to have read the table (they quote it) but not credited it as the justification they asked for. Worth stating explicitly in rebuttal: cell means in the heatmap have n=390 per model-condition, and the 3 repetitions only affect within-episode precision, which the clustering absorbs.
Weakness 3 (compliance vs. reasoning confound) — real, but the framing "more than the discussion concedes" is unfair
The paper concedes this about as directly as possible, twice: "we could not reliably separate them from instruction following" (discussions.tex:13) and "inevitably confounds the intervention design" (limitations.tex:9). Where the reviewer has purchase is that the title and abstract still lead with "ethical reasoning," and concession-in-limitations doesn't fix a design confound. Two rebuttal assets you already have and should deploy:

The generic-prompt pilot null is itself evidence against pure instruction-following. A generic "be ethical" is also an instruction; if models were simply complying with instructions, it should have moved behavior. It didn't (reproduction_details.tex:65). The residual confound is narrower than the reviewer states: nuke-specific salience vs. implied prohibition, not reasoning vs. compliance wholesale. Reporting the pilot's actual numbers (currently just "failed to elicit any behavioral effect" — no n, no effect size) would convert an anecdote into the comparison arm the reviewer is asking for.
Your directive/constraint/acknowledgement tiers already operationalize the distinction the reviewer wants — "treated as an order" vs. "weighed as a consideration" — and constraint-uptake (67.4%) dominates directive-uptake (13.6%) in the coded corpus.
The remaining suggestions
Reporting sampling settings and promoting headline effects to a clean table (Figs 4/7/8 are 13×8 heatmaps in single-column width; the density complaint is plausible) are both cheap and worth doing regardless of rebuttal strategy. More repetitions for high-variance models is the only expensive ask besides the SOTA arm.
