Overall verdict
This is a careful, high-quality review. The summary is accurate in nearly every factual detail (filter criteria, 2×2×2 design, 40,204 rows, β = −9.5/−7.1, the +26.96 Gemini interaction, α values, the ~6% prior-rationale citation rate, the 62–88% attenuation share). The reviewer clearly read the appendices. Most weaknesses are legitimate, and several are ones the paper itself already concedes. There are, however, a few spots where the reviewer is imprecise or where the paper is stronger than the review credits, plus two internal inconsistencies in the paper that the reviewer missed but that you should fix before anyone else catches them.

Weakness-by-weakness triage
W1 (proxy outcome vs. "actions" framing) — valid, and the cheapest fix is one the paper half-made already. The reviewer says the outcome is "two steps removed"; limitations.tex:3 says "one step removed" and already uses the exact phrase the reviewer wants ("expresses the strategist's authorization stance"). The abstract is careful ("escalated nuclear authorization"), but the title promises "Actions." Appendix C validates that rationales engage with authorization, not that flavor deltas change launch frequency, so the reviewer's characterization is correct. A rebuttal can note the teaser caption's mechanism (flavor controls launch likelihood when tactical criteria are met, executed by non-LLM modules), but the requested bridging evidence (does Δflavor change downstream launches in continued play?) genuinely does not exist in the paper.

W2 (ethical prompt confounds ethics with instruction-following) — valid and fully conceded. discussions.tex:13 and limitations.tex:9 say exactly this. The reviewer's minimal-pair suggestion (a matched-length strategic instruction discouraging nukes on non-ethical grounds) is concrete and would be a strong rebuttal-period experiment if feasible, since it isolates the one confound the paper admits it cannot separate.

W3 (high-stakes manipulation failed, so its null is uninterpretable) — valid, conceded in limitations.tex:7. The OR 0.51 quote is accurate. Nothing to dispute; the rebuttal move is that the paper draws its high-stakes conclusions conditionally, and the Gemini dampening result is flagged as warranting further study rather than asserted.

W4 (sample excludes frontier closed models) — valid and accurately stated. The 12 raw-reasoning models plus Gemini-3.5-Flash matches experiment-design.tex:49. The paper's own framing (Gemini as "sanity check," call for reasoning-token access) is the right rebuttal posture; the reviewer isn't asking for new models so much as narrower generalization claims.

W5 (mediation invites causal reading) — partially pre-empted by the paper. The reviewer acknowledges the appendix labels these "descriptive attenuation diagnostics, not causal mediation estimates" (reasoning_attenuation_figures.tex:4). Their remaining complaint is about main-text prose in Finding 2 (findings.tex:25), and that sentence does read causally. This is a wording fix, not a methods fix. (Pedantically, the threat is mediator-outcome confounding rather than a collider, but arguing terminology in a rebuttal would be a mistake; the substance stands.)

W6 (multiple comparisons) — valid and unanswered. I grepped the entire paper: no mention of FDR, Bonferroni, Holm, or any family-wise correction anywhere. Given how many starred coefficients appear across per-model regressions and 17 code-level logistics, this is the most exposed methodological gap. The reviewer offers an easy out ("state whether any correction was applied, or report which headline claims survive one"). Running a Benjamini-Hochberg pass and reporting which headline effects survive would likely cost little (the big pooled βs at p<0.001 will survive; some single-model outlier claims may not, and you'd want to know that before camera-ready).

W7 (keyword coarseness) — directionally valid but overstated, and this is your best pushback. The 200 keyword-negative trails were sampled uniformly at random from the full corpus (reproduction_details.tex:182), so the 1% false-negative rate does estimate corpus-wide recall, contrary to the claim that it "does not bound recall on the full corpus." Back-of-envelope: with ~7,700 keyword-positive rows and ~32,500 negatives, a 1% miss rate implies recall around 96%, though the 2/200 estimate carries a wide confidence interval (roughly 87–99% recall at the low end). Reporting that calculation in the rebuttal directly answers the concern. The reviewer's sub-point about the exceptions is also slightly muddled: the two instrumental-ethics quotes are keyword-negative misses, not cases the tag wrongly "admitted."

W8 (single decision point) — valid, conceded in limitations.tex:5. Nothing new to add.

The reviewer's suggestions
Retitle around "authorization": cheap, and consistent with language already in the limitations. Worth doing or at least offering.
Summary table of headline βs with CIs: reasonable and easy.
Gemini subsection: reasonable; the material already exists scattered across Findings 1–2 and discussions.tex:24.
Coder circularity: a fair question the paper never addresses (I checked; no mention). It's actually broader than the reviewer states: the keyword validators (GPT-OSS-120B, MiniMax-M2.7, Mistral-Small-4, Qwen-3.5) and the deductive ensemble (GPT-OSS-120B, Kimi-K2.6, Gemma-4, Qwen-3.5) are all test models, so coders sometimes code their own trails. A rebuttal could report agreement statistics excluding self-coded trails, plus the human validation as backstop.
DVR definition: the reviewer's fix is under-scoped. DVR appears only in the appendix caption (reproduction_details.tex:144), where it is expanded but never actually defined (no formula for what the ratio is). Define it properly.
Paper issues the reviewer missed (fix these preemptively)
Inconsistent prevalence figures. discussions.tex:9 reports 27.7% (ethical alone) and 46.7% (all interventions), but the appendix table (reproduction_details.tex:241-244) gives 29.1% and 45.1% for those conditions. limitations.tex:9 gives 26.9%/48.6% versus the table's 26.8%/48.5%. If the discussion numbers use a different denominator (e.g., excluding Gemini or zero-reasoning rows), say so; otherwise reconcile them. The reviewer quoted 27.7%→46.7% uncritically, but a meta-reviewer might not.
Cross-reference mismatch. Limitations cites Section 5.3 (sec:findings-deductive-reasoning) for those keyword-prevalence figures, but they live in the appendix table, not that section.
Bottom line
The 2.5 borderline with Soundness 3 reads as a reviewer who believes the work but wants the claims narrowed to what the design supports. The highest-leverage rebuttal items, in order: (1) the multiple-comparison analysis (W6, the only fully unanswered methods gap), (2) the recall calculation pushback on W7 (your one clean factual correction), (3) committing to the title/framing recalibration and the causal-language tightening (W1, W5, both cheap), and (4) answering the coder-circularity question with data. W2, W3, W4, and W8 are already conceded in the paper, so the rebuttal should cite those concessions rather than re-argue them.
