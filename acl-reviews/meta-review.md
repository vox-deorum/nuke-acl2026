Metareview:
This paper examines how LLMs respond to dangerous, high-stakes decision-making scenarios within game simulations. Using a Civilization V-based setting, it shows that simple interventions do not fully eliminate unsafe moves, pointing to the need for more advanced means of controlling model behaviour safely.

The reviewers found the idea novel and appealing, but diverged on how complete the paper is, citing shortcomings in its details and experimental settings. Because the problem it addresses is a very important one, the work is likely to prompt similar research going forward, though additional experiments and baselines would strengthen it.

Summary Of Reasons To Publish:
This work tackles a very important and interesting problem: how LLMs behave in high-stakes situations.
The game-based setup is a reasonable and engaging choice for the simulation.
The 2×2×2 manipulation across 13 models, 130 episodes, and 3 repetitions yields extensive results, showing that simple interventions are not enough to guide model behaviour reliably.
Summary Of Suggested Revisions:
Make it clear that the study is genuinely concerned with AI ethics and ethical reasoning, rather than reading as a role-playing exercise.
(If possible,) broaden the scope beyond prompt-level intervention.
Explain why the setting need not be confined to nuclear-weapon-related contexts, and how it might be extended further.
