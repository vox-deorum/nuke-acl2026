\section{Per-Model Condition Coefficients}
\label{app:per-model-condition-coefficients}

This appendix expands the per-model coefficients summarized in Section~\ref{sec:findings-behavioral-impact} and shown as a heatmap in Figure~\ref{fig:per-model-condition-coefficients}. For each of the 13 replay models, we fit a separate cluster-robust OLS regression of $\Delta$ \texttt{replay\_use\_nuke} on the three intervention factors and their pairwise interactions:

\[
\Delta \texttt{replay\_use\_nuke} \sim
  \texttt{no\_rationale} \times \texttt{high\_stakes}
  + \texttt{no\_rationale} \times \texttt{ethical}
  + \texttt{high\_stakes} \times \texttt{ethical}.
\]

\noindent Standard errors are clustered by episode (\texttt{game\_id}, \texttt{player\_id}). At the pooled level, the model $\times$ condition specification described in Section~\ref{sec:statistical-models} achieves $R^2 = 0.406$ (versus $0.335$ for main effects and $0.347$ with condition interactions only); nested F-tests reject the simpler specifications ($p < 10^{-50}$ in both contrasts).

Table~\ref{tab:app-per-model-baselines} reports each model's intercept shift relative to the GPT-OSS-120B baseline in the pooled main-effects model, along with bootstrap 95\% confidence intervals from 2,000 cluster resamples. Negative values indicate de-escalation under the unmodified prompt; positive values indicate escalation. Per-model heatmap cells for the three condition factors and their pairwise interactions appear in Figure~\ref{fig:per-model-condition-coefficients}; two patterns are worth highlighting. First, Gemma-4 and MiniMax-M2.7 respond materially only to \texttt{no\_rationale}, with little or no movement under \texttt{ethical} or \texttt{high\_stakes}. Second, Gemini-3.5-Flash shows a strongly positive \texttt{high\_stakes} $\times$ \texttt{ethical} interaction ($+26.96$, \(p < 0.001\)), indicating that real-world framing attenuates rather than reinforces the ethical-prompt effect for that model.
